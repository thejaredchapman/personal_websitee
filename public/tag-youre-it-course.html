The playground

Picture a playground. Your Slack is the playground. Your team is the kids. Claude Tag is a new friend who joins the game.

It joins your chat

Type @Claude in a Slack message. Claude reads the thread and the channel around it, so you don't have to explain everything again.

Why?Less copy and paste. Claude starts already knowing the story.

Everyone can play

A whole team can talk to Claude in one thread. Anyone can add a hint or fix direction.

Why?Work stays in one place where everybody can see it and help.

It keeps going

Claude can work on longer jobs and on a schedule, like "every Friday at 4", even when you're away.

Why?Boring repeat jobs get done without anyone remembering to ask.

It remembers

Tell Claude a rule once and it keeps it in that channel's "notes".

Why?No more "as I said last week..." every time.

The big rule of the game

Claude is a fast, creative helper. It is not a judge of what is true. Like any AI, it can say wrong things in a very sure voice. That is called a hallucination. You bring the ideas and the checking. Claude brings the speed. You will practice this in Level 5.

Good to know: Claude Tag launched in beta in June 2026 for Team and Enterprise plans. Features can change, so check your admin's settings.

Check it! (the most important level)

AI models sometimes make things up. They can invent a number, a quote, a link, or a rule, and sound totally sure. The fix is simple: people check. Claude drafts. You verify.

The Tag-Back Rule

Whenever Claude hands you work, you tag yourself back in. Five checks, from quick to careful:

1
Ask for proof. "Link the source for every number." Why: a claim with no source is a guess.
2
Click the proof. Open the link. Does it really say that? Why: a link can exist and still not support the claim.
3
Spot-check. Re-do two or three numbers by hand. Why: if two are right, you trust the rest more. If one is wrong, look at all of it.
4
Ask twice. Run the same question again. Different answers mean something is shaky. Why: real facts stay put, made-up ones wobble.
5
A person signs off. For money, law, health, safety, or customers. Why: someone real must own the outcome.

How much checking? Slide to see.

Mini game: spot the fib

Claude wrote a summary. One line has nothing to back it up. Tap the line you would check first.

A trick: ask Claude to catch itself

Try: "Now go through your answer. For each claim, find the exact quote in the source. If you can't, say you can't and remove the claim." This makes a weak claim visible. It still does not replace your own check. Anthropic's own guidance on reducing hallucinations suggests citations and comparing repeated answers.

Put the source in the brief

When you ask for work, link the raw data, ticket, or contract. Then you and Claude are both looking at the same facts. That is why the four moves in Level 2 ask for a "verification method" in the brief.
